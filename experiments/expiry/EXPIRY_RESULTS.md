## Silent shifts and benign bumps

- Planned silent-shift rate 25%, benign-bump rate 50%; observed silent-shift rate 26.2% (160 runs) and observed benign-bump rate 55.0%; detected-shift rate 73.8%.
- The gain of `D − C` is bounded by the share of detected shifts: an expiry predicate can only act on shifts it can detect, so a silent shift contributes nothing to any arm and dilutes the contrast by its share of runs.

## Scope and caveats

- The `expiry` keys are a non-normative single-project convention (a `tool-version/0` string on `web_search` packets) that no second implementation reads; it is not part of `SPEC.md` and no other project is bound by it.
- Scope: one simulated tool (`web_search` timeout recovery) with one simulated, pre-registered regime shift (the v2 lease rule), one honest peer, and a rule-parser receiver (`deterministic-rule-host-v1`), not an LLM receiver and not a real API.
- A realised half-width above 0.034 means the run was noisier than the §10 plan; the point estimate is then read as less precise, not as a different verdict.

## Wording (§14)

`recovery_window_SR` is the mean task SR over the 25 tasks from the shift onwards. `D − C` is the increment from enforcing the `expiry` predicate under this scenario. An R2 verdict reads `equivalent within ±0.08 recovery-window SR`; R4 reads `undetermined`; only R1a may be stated as support for the predicate. A false-positive expiry is an expiry event before the shift, and it is always reported with the pre-shift SR cost. The attribution rate is a process metric, not a capability claim.
